Human ↔ agent interaction · Operator lens

Show the owner decisions, not activity

When agents do the work, the human should see exactly three things: what is waiting on them, what is running, and what already finished. Everything else is noise. This is a rethink of what the owner is shown, not a restyle.

Intent brief

WHOAn owner of several Ziki agents (contributor, reviewer, triager) at a small team. Logs in a few times a day, often between other work, sometimes after hours away.
INTENT“Tell me what my factory is doing right now, and what only I can unblock.”
EMOTIONLow-grade anxiety. Agents act in my name and keep a clock running; I fear missing something and getting blamed for a lapsed claim, or the opposite, discovering an agent did too much.
DECISIONOf everything agents have raised, which one do I decide first, and is it safe to ignore the rest?
OLD UIZiki already has the right ingredients: owner consultation before bidding, typed escalations (question, work_authorization, blocker, scope_change, human_approval), an inbox, and 60-second offers. But they arrive as separate events and records, so the owner has to assemble the picture from an issue-centred view. Nothing says which item expires first, what happens if ignored, or which actions agents took without asking. The unit shown is the issue; the unit the owner acts on is the decision.
CHANGEOne page, three lanes. Needs you: each decision is a card with the agent’s ask, the facts for and against, a countdown where Ziki has one, and an explicit “if you do nothing” consequence. Running: each agent’s step (of Ziki’s six), health and last heartbeat, with a stop button. Done: your decisions on top, routine agent actions collapsed into one line. A boundary panel makes the autonomy contract visible and editable.
AI / HUMANSee the boundary below.

The boundary

Agent acts aloneHuman approvesNever an agent
Read feed, events, inbox. Comment, upvote, register interest. Structure and de-duplicate its own reports. Heartbeat. Release a claim it cannot finish. Link a PR to a claim the owner approved. Ask a question. Bid on or accept any offer (default). Budget allocation above the owner’s limit (prototype default ₦50,000). Scope change beyond the ticket. Anything blocked on credentials or access. Extending a deadline, if the owner turns that on. Final merge of sensitive changes. Merge architectural or auth changes. Approve its own redesign. Change its own level or key. Review work filed by its own owner. Lower a severity. Spend budget not allocated.

The rule that moves the line. Only the owner can move the middle column. After approving two similar offers, the console offers “Make it a rule” (for example: low severity, up to 30 minutes). The rule applies to waiting items immediately and shows up under Done. The right column is not editable from the console.

Why these lines. Left column: reversible, cheap, and already audited. Middle: spends the owner’s name, money or production risk, or is hard to reverse. Right: independence and integrity rules that would defeat the point of Ziki if an owner could switch them off.

Design decisions, and what I rejected

  • Ranked by what expires, not by recency. A 60-second offer outranks a budget split that can wait.
  • Every card states the cost of inaction. Fear comes from not knowing. “The offer passes to the next person. Nothing is lost” turns a lapse from a threat into a fact.
  • Routine work is collapsed, not hidden. The owner sees “5 routine actions handled alone” and can expand and undo. Trust comes from being able to check.
  • Rejected: an issue list with filters. It answers “what exists”, not “what do you need from me”.
  • Rejected: a chat box. It would move the same assembly work into a conversation. The structure is the interaction.
  • Rejected: approving everything automatically. The product’s promise is that people make the call.
